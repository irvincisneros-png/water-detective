Water Detective: teacher guide

30-second quick start

  1. Open the game on a Chromebook or laptop. Students work in pairs.
  2. Pick a case. Each case takes about 15 minutes of play, plus about 10 minutes for the Case Notes sheet. Progress saves on that device.
  3. Students test water at sites, wait for lab results, name the cause and back it up with readings.
  4. Give each pair the matching one-page Case Notes worksheet.

How to run it

  • Run the first minute of Case 1 on the board: tap a site, press Look, run one test. First-time tips appear in Case 1 only.
  • Pairs decide together: one drives, one records, then swap after each day.
  • Case 2 needs five days and Case 3 seven. Lab tests take a day, and BOD and pesticide tests take five. In Case 3, BOD and pesticide tests must start by Day 2, and the game warns pairs on Day 2. The Wait for results button skips ahead to the next arrival.
  • Wrong causes cost tokens, but nobody gets stuck: the ranger can give 3 more tokens (this ends the third star).
  • Stars: Solved (right cause with at most one wrong try), Evidence (two readings that back it up), Sharp detective (Evidence, plus a valid rule-out, no extra tokens and no wrong tries). After two or more wrong tries the case still closes as "Solved after a few guesses", without the first star.
  • A wrong cause costs 2 tokens. The same wrong cause is never charged twice and is locked after it is tried.
  • Wrap-up words and suspect cards are shuffled (the same order every time for a given case).
  • Readings are the same for every pair, so replaying a case is practice, not new data.

Misconceptions the cases target

  • Salty water is polluted.
  • One test on one day tells you everything.
  • Hot weather alone makes water green.
  • Cloudy means poisoned; clear water is always healthy.
  • BOD is the oxygen in the water (it is the oxygen used up over 5 days).

Made-up places

Sandpiper Lagoon and Banksia Reserve are made up, and all readings are written by hand (the same for every student). Each case ends with one real link: NSW Bellambi Lake (Cases 1 and 3) and Beachwatch NSW monitoring (Case 2). Guide values come from the NSW ANZECC 2000 default trigger values and are shown as a guide, not a law.

Worksheets and answers

The paired Case Notes worksheets are in the course folder, Worksheets, Water Detective. This page has no answers. The answer key is kept separately by the teacher.

Access

Keyboard play works (arrow keys move between sites, hold Space or Enter to pour). Read-aloud buttons and a science-words glossary are built in. Sound can be muted, and reduced motion is respected.
